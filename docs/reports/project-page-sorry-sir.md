## Short project description

A cleaner cracks a wall. By evening the crack is art, and the price is climbing.

---

## ✨ Context

The second film from ILAG Studio, made for the Higgsfield Global Film Festival.

It began as a joke somebody refused to let go of: if a signature can make a urinal art,
what does a gallery do with damage? The whole film is the answer played straight — no
winks, no narrator, nobody in the room ever admits it is absurd.

Made over roughly a month by a very small team, with the discipline that usually goes
into a much bigger one: one canon page for every character, one look written before a
frame existed, and a rule that changing any reference meant re-shooting everything built
on it.

## 🎭 Narrative

A cleaner named Dupe knocks a painting off a wall and leaves a hand-sized crack in the
plaster. He phones a workman to fill it. Before the plaster arrives, a visitor stops in
front of the crack, gets angry, and then decides he likes it.

By the time the room agrees, the crack has a brass plaque, a price, and an artist who
did not make it. Collectors bid. A critic explains what it means. The price goes from two
million to a hundred. The wall is cut out of the building and crated. Everyone is
perfectly sincere.

**The message, if it needs one:** value is assigned, not found — and once a room has
agreed on a price, the truth of how the thing got there stops mattering.

It ends where it started, with the man holding the mop. He has learned exactly one
lesson, and he picks up a hammer.

**For:** anyone who likes deadpan satire played dead straight, symmetrical frames, and
being trusted to get the joke without help.

## 🎨 Visuals

**Reference:** Wes Anderson, openly — flat dead-on symmetry, long lenses, frontal
staging, a camera that never comments. Institutional retro-futurism for the museum:
chromium trumpet columns in matched pairs, orange cove light washing the ceiling,
polished terracotta terrazzo, warm cream stone.

**Palette:** a warm neutral architecture holding deeply saturated costume colour.
Every visitor wears one colour and commits to it — magenta fur, cobalt, chrome yellow,
oxblood, green leather. The building stays quiet so the people can be loud.

**Colour direction**, written before the first frame and carried in all 77 prompt sheets:
warm shadow against cold white — amber-orange highlights and mids, whites pushed
slightly cool, shadows deep red-brown, halation around every lamp. Photographed, not
rendered: fine grain, faint gate weave, slight colour fringing.

**The single hardest visual rule:** one face per reference plate, and a written canon
page every prompt had to agree with, so that fifteen characters stay themselves across
eight minutes.

## 🎬 Production

**Schedule:** roughly one month, start to delivery.

**Generated on Higgsfield.** Seedance 2.5 for most shots, Seedance 2.0 Fast where a
shorter clip served, GPT Image 2 for the reference plates every scene was built against.
Assembly, colour and finishing outside the platform, as the rules permit.

**Locations** are all built as reference plates and reused: the great hall, the hero
wall, the museum exterior, the artist's house. Nothing was shot; nothing was matted in
from stock.

**Crew:** a director who owns every creative call, one editor, and a small production
pipeline that keeps the cast, the props and the prompts in agreement with each other.

**Logistics worth naming:** the film runs 105 shots. The cast list, the wardrobe, the
props and the locked dialogue live in one canon document, and every prompt sheet is
checked against it before anything is generated. That is the only reason a film made one
clip at a time still looks like one film.

## 📝 Notes

**Decisions worth keeping:**

- **One face per reference plate.** Three plates were lost to content scanning in a
  single day, each with more than one person in it. Splitting them fixed it permanently,
  and location plates now contain no people at all — which also stops a scene inheriting
  a wardrobe that has since changed.
- **A canon cast page from day one.** Two versions of one character had shipped into an
  early cut before anyone noticed, because no single page existed where the split would
  have been visible.
- **A reference beats the words.** When a bound plate and the written description
  disagree, the model follows the plate. Every prompt therefore describes what the plate
  already shows, as a fallback for when a reference silently fails to attach.
- **Re-shoot everything downstream of a changed reference.** One location plate fed
  twelve scenes; replacing it meant re-shooting most of the film. Worth knowing before
  ordering the plate, not after.
- **Colour last.** Grading a cut that is about to change is work done twice.
