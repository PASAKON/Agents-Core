## 1. The wave taller than a skyscraper (Lituya Bay, Alaska, 9 July 1958) ⭐ recommended
A night-time earthquake drops a mountainside into a narrow Alaskan bay, and three small fishing boats at anchor
face a 524 m wave, the tallest ever recorded. A father and his 7-year-old son ride it out; one boat is swept over the
forest at the bay's mouth.
- Why it can win: it is a tsunami (the CEO's pick, Q-…-ab36) with a number viewers repeat ("taller than the Empire
  State Building"), survivors' own accounts, and few people on screen (two boats, wide water and mountains: AI's strong shots).
- Sources: USGS, "The 1958 Lituya Bay landslide and tsunami"; Miller, D.J. (1960), "Giant waves in Lituya Bay, Alaska", USGS Professional Paper 354-C; Wikipedia "1958 Lituya Bay earthquake and megatsunami".

## 2. The man the volcano spared (Saint-Pierre, Martinique, 8 May 1902)
Mount Pelée erupts and in about a minute a cloud of gas and ash kills nearly everyone in Saint-Pierre, the island's
largest town. One of the few survivors is a prisoner in a thick-walled cell, found burned but alive days later.
- Why it can win: one man, one cell, a whole town lost: a hook that tells itself; ironic turn (the prison saved him).
- Risk: ~28,000 dead; the town in its final hours needs care under rule 6 (no gore, no mocking).
- Sources: Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, Pelée; Wikipedia "1902 eruption of Mount Pelée" and "Ludger Sylbaris"; Heilprin, A. (1903), "Mont Pelée and the Tragedy of Martinique".

## 3. The fire nobody remembers (Peshtigo, Wisconsin, 8 October 1871)
On the same night as the Great Chicago Fire, a firestorm burns a logging town in Wisconsin and kills far more people;
survivors live through the night standing neck-deep in the river while the town burns.
- Why it can win: "the deadliest fire in American history that nobody remembers" is the same promise as the model's
  "forgotten survivors"; fire was named by the CEO (M0b: "ไฟไฟ").
- Risk: ~1,200-2,400 dead, many burned; fire on people needs the most care under rule 6.
- Sources: Wisconsin Historical Society, "Peshtigo Fire"; National Weather Service, "The Peshtigo Fire of 1871"; Pernin, P. (1874), "The Great Peshtigo Fire: An Eyewitness Account".
