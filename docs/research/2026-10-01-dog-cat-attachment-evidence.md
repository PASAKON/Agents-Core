## D1. Is a dog's tie to its owner a real attachment in the scientific sense?

**Answer:** Dogs exhibit operational attachment to owners, showing an owner-specific secure base effect during problem solving and infant-like secure versus insecure attachment classifications.

## D2. Why does a dog follow its caregiver into something it fears (water, a flood, a gate)?

**Answer:** Dogs follow caregivers into feared situations because the owner acts as a physiological safe-haven stress buffer and provides communicative reassurance via social referencing.

## D3. Why would a dog run and hide (under a sofa) when it hears a phrase that always comes before a bath?

**Answer (CMO-corrected):** No study here tested a word that predicts a bath. What the rows support: dogs with pre-existing fear of vet handling show reduced posture, and that fear can be reduced by training (D3-1); fear is common in companion dogs (D3-2). 'Hides when it hears the bath phrase' is conditioned avoidance by inference from general learning theory, not a finding of these rows.

## D4. Why would a dog leave the caregiver who bathes her and go sleep in another household member's room?

**Answer (CMO-corrected):** No study here tested a dog leaving the person who bathes her to sleep in another person's room. The rows support only: dogs securely attached to a child were also securely attached to the parent (D4-1, 9 dogs, a therapy-programme sample), and, with the owner and a household dog competing, dogs stayed closer to the owner (D4-2). The bedroom move is our reading, not a finding.

## D5. How does a dog react to a household member who ignores her (does not talk to or touch her)?

**Answer:** Dogs are sensitive to human attentional orientation, significantly increasing communicative facial expressions and gaze alternations when a person is attentive and adjusting their position when ignored.

## C1. Do cats form attachment bonds with people?

**Answer:** While cats discriminate owners from strangers through differential vocalization and exhibit rebound contact-seeking after separation, evidence that their bond constitutes an Ainsworth-style secure attachment remains inconsistent.
