## What changed

He was shown the three options and said, in his own words: he likes **bold, saturated colour**,

**Read what this means for your brief, because it inverts part of it:**

This is not a failure of A — it answered the brief it was given. The brief was incomplete.

**On creative judgement the CEO's eye is final.** That rule was in AGENTS.md from the start and it

## What the reference actually does, mechanically

Do not take "more saturated" as the instruction — an adjective is not a direction, and a global
saturation lift is not this look. What characterises it:

| Element | What the reference does |
|---|---|
| **Palette** | **Narrow, not wide.** A few hues dominate; everything else is pushed toward them or made near-neutral. Saturation is high *within* the palette, not everywhere. |
| **Shadows** | **Lifted and milky. NOT crushed.** This directly contradicts A/B/C, all of which deepen shadow density. This is the single biggest structural change. |
| **Mid contrast** | **Low.** Flat, even, poster-like. The image does not go punchy — it goes graphic. |
| **Highlights** | **Pastel and creamy.** They roll off into tinted cream, never clipped white. |
| **Anchor** | A **complementary pair** holding the frame — pink against teal, red against purple. |

## The honest constraint, which you should confirm or refute with your own eyes

Grand Budapest's colour is **production design** — the hotel is physically pink. Our sets are a
cream/beige gallery with chrome pillars and orange cove lighting. **A grade cannot invent
production design**, and pushing cream walls toward pink will look like a fault, not a choice.

**But the film already owns an Anderson-ish palette in its costumes**, and that is where the look
should live:

- crimson fur coat · cobalt blue overcoat · green leather coat · deep maroon suit
- bleached-yellow hair · white service uniform · the gold plaque · blue-white TV glow

The proposal we want tested: **let the costumes and the props carry the saturation, hold the
architecture as a warm neutral field behind them**, and use the cream-amber room against the
cobalt coat as the complementary anchor. That is a real Anderson structure and it is achievable on
this footage.

**If you look at the frames and think that is wrong, say so and propose what is right.** You can
see the pictures and we are reasoning from three still references of a different film.

## What we want back

**One new look, `D`, in the stylised direction** — built the same way you built A/B/C, delivered to
the same standard: cube file, per-clip corrections if needed, exact values, the Lua route, frames.

**Push it further than feels correct.** If D comes back and the CEO says "still too subtle", we
have spent a session learning nothing. The failure mode we are guarding against is restraint, not
excess. He can always be walked back; he has already told us the quiet version is not it.

**If you think there are two genuinely different ways to read "Grand Budapest on this footage",
give us both — D1 and D2.** Same rule as last time: real alternatives beat one safe answer.

## Judge D against these, and tell us honestly where it fails

1. Does it look **designed** — would a viewer notice the colour as a choice?
2. Do **skin tones still read as human skin**? This is the one hard limit; stylised is fine,
   sick-looking is not.
3. Do the **costume colours separate** — is the crimson fur still distinct from the maroon suit?
4. Do **S22 (2.5) and S21 (2.0 Fast) still sit as one film**? The answer that made A work must
   survive the new look, or D needs its own per-clip layer.
5. Is the **gold plaque still gold**? It carries text the audience reads.
