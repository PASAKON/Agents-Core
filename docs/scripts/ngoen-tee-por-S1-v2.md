## 4. tig-scene-engine chain check

**SCENE: Scene 1 — "เดี๋ยวโทรกลับ" (morning service, the first crack)**

Story goal (from series premise engine): สมชาย's goal is to keep the loan
and its cost hidden from ต้น permanently — real giving as silence, never as
disclosure.

**Goal** — สมชาย's scene goal: get through this ordinary morning shift
without the concealment slipping. Causal link: this is the *first* stage of
the multi-scene sequence that runs to the reveal (old EP1 shots ~37–45) —
remove this scene and the audience has no baseline "normal" to see crack,
so the later reveal lands with less weight. Passes the removal test. ✅

**Obstacle** — the lender's repeated calls landing *during* service, in
front of his son. What's at risk: not the debt itself yet — at this stage
it's narrower: **the lie holding.** Scale: **local** (this stage/scene —
can he get through one shift without his son clocking it), but it
foreshadows the **global** obstacle (the whole four-year concealment) since
the calls are shown as recurring, not one-off. ✅ genuinely jeopardized —
his son is already counting the calls by line 3.

**Tactic** — สมชาย's tactic is verbal deflection three times in a row:
silence the phone / call it an old customer / redirect to his son's job
search / answer too fast on the medicine question. This is a reasonable bet
**on his current knowledge** — he has no reason yet to think ต้น suspects
anything specific, so a light deflection should hold. The outcome **returns
information**: it doesn't hold — his son notices the strain anyway ("พ่อ...
วันนี้พ่อดูไม่ค่อยดีเลยนะ"). That's a real narrowing: สมชาย now knows (and we
know) that flat denial isn't going to be enough going forward, which is
exactly the pressure the later scenes escalate. ✅ forced by the jeopardy,
not a wheel-spin — each deflection changes what's needed next (topic
change → reassurance → instant "it's fine" → finally food, an
escalating retreat, not a repeated identical move).

**Reversal** — form 1 (**my own action flips on me**): สมชาย's whole
tactic *is* "act normal so nothing looks wrong" — and that tactic is what
produces the tell. The calmer and faster he answers, the more visibly wrong
he reads to his son. Acting normal is the thing that outs him. One reversal
lands cleanly inside this single scene (shots 8→9); it also opens a larger
sequence-level jeopardy (the concealment itself) that will need its own
reversal later, further downstream — flagged here as the parent sequence,
not resolved yet. ✅ ≥1 reversal present at the scene level.

**Value Shift** — before-verdict (shots 1–6): ordinary tired dad, brushing
off a nuisance call, nothing more. After-verdict (shots 7–10): something is
being deliberately kept from both of us — not just fatigue, a held-back
fact, and he is bad at holding it. That's a real re-judgment: the audience
goes from "he's fine, just busy" to "he is lying, and he knows he's bad at
it." Not inert — nameable before/after. ✅

**WEAKEST POINT:** the obstacle's stakes are still abstract at this scale —
"the lie holding" is real but low-visible jeopardy compared to what's
coming (a beating, a four-year debt). That's intentional for shot 1 of a
much longer sequence, not a flaw, but it means this scene's reversal is
*necessary, not sufficient* — Scene 2 has to raise the stakes fast or the
sequence goes slack. See §5 below.

## 5. What Scene 2 now has to do

Because Scene 1 ends on an **unresolved, still-abstract** threat (an
unnamed caller, a vague "it's fine"), Scene 2 carries the next causal link
and must do two things through dialogue, not picture:

1. **Give the caller a voice and a name**, or at minimum close the gap
   between "old customer" (the lie) and what the audience now suspects is
   false — the next reversal in this sequence needs to raise the obstacle
   from *local* (will the lie hold today) toward *global* (what happens if
   it doesn't). This is the natural place for เชิด (@lender_cherd) to
   either call again on-mic, or appear — but per the same dialogue-first
   rule, his first line has to do real narrative work, not just menace.
2. **Escalate ต้น's suspicion into an actual tactic of his own** — right
   now he's only noticing; Scene 2 should have him *act* on it (check the
   phone, ask someone else, follow his father) so he has a goal-driven
   reason to be present at whatever confrontation comes next, rather than
   stumbling into it. That keeps him a participant in the engine, not a
   bystander the plot happens near.

ย่าประนอม is intentionally **not** in Scene 1 — the witness device is
reserved for later (per the original scene map, upstairs). Holding her back
here is a choice, not an oversight: introducing a character who can't speak
inside a scene whose whole method is "the dialogue must carry the scene"
would either force her into words she shouldn't have, or waste a shot she
can't carry alone. She belongs where her silence is itself the point.
