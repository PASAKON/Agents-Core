WHAT THIS SCENE IS ABOUT — READ THIS, IT DECIDES EVERY CHOICE
================================================================

This is the SECOND time the mother knocks on this door. The first time she came in her own old clothes and the door opened a hand's width and closed again.

THIS TIME SHE IS WEARING VALDER, AND THE DOOR OPENS HALF WAY.

Half way. Not all the way. She gets to step inside and stand in the receiving room — and NOBODY ASKS HER TO SIT DOWN. She stands, holding herself very straight, in an enormous empty expensive room, while the neighbour walks around her.

THE ONE GESTURE THIS SCENE TURNS ON: the neighbour reaches out and TOUCHES THE FABRIC OF THE MOTHER'S SLEEVE WITH ONE FINGERTIP, the way you would check the quality of a thing in a shop. Then she says one flat line — and the line is about the garment, not about the woman wearing it.

THE POINT: the mother has not been accepted. HER CLOTHES HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED. She is a stand for a garment. And it worked, which is what makes it unbearable — it means she has to keep buying.

THE DOOR IS THE MEASURING DEVICE OF THIS FILM. Visit one: a hand's width. Visit two: half open. Visit three: open before she knocks. Shoot this so the audience can feel the door doing that work.

NOBODY IS CRUEL. The neighbour is pleasant throughout. There is no sneering, no contempt, no raised voice.

WHAT PALE MEANS HERE
================================================================

Both women are PALE — but pale means ORDINARY HUMAN INDOOR PALLOR, the skin of a person who spends life indoors and does not see the sun. IT IS NEVER GREEN, NEVER GREY, NEVER BLUE, NEVER SILVER. Normal warm undertone, visible pores, real human skin texture. Think of a clerk at the end of a long winter. NOT a creature, NOT a corpse, NOT anything from science fiction or horror.

The neighbour is paler. The mother is on her way there — a little less warm than she was, not yet all the way. The audience should feel that she is changing, without being told.

Both women must read as REAL PHOTOGRAPHED HUMAN BEINGS.

STYLE — APPLIES TO EVERY SHOT
================================================================

ERA: a retrofuturist world — the future as people in the past imagined it. Swept aerodynamic curves, cantilevers, moulded plastic, chromium, terrazzo, formica, starburst motifs, boomerang shapes, tapered splayed legs. THERE IS NOTHING DIGITAL ANYWHERE. No screens, no LEDs, no digital numerals, no cables. An invented place — no country and no year is identifiable.

COLOUR IS THE CLASS SYSTEM: wealth is DEEP SATURATED COLOUR IN LARGE FLAT PLANES, and EMPTINESS. The parlour is drenched in colour and almost bare of objects. That emptiness is the luxury.

NOBODY LOOKS AT THE CAMERA. Every gaze is aimed at a person or an object inside the frame.

ALL SPOKEN DIALOGUE IN THIS FILM IS IN ENGLISH.

================================================================
WHAT MARKS A PERSON AS ONE OF THEM — READ THIS, IT REPLACES AN EARLIER RULE
================================================================

**THE GARMENT IS WHAT READS ON SIGHT. THE GOLD V IS A TRADEMARK, AND ANYONE MAY HAVE ONE.**

A Valder coat is unmistakable on sight — floor-length or near it, built from large flat panels of several intensely saturated colours meeting at hard edges, deliberately asymmetric, worn with matching coloured gloves and matching coloured shoes and a scarf at the throat. **Wearing one is what says you belong**, because it is instantly legible across a street. The trademark is not doing that job; the coat is.

**THE GOLD V IS VALDER'S TRADEMARK.** It is stamped, cast, embossed or printed on everything he makes and sells — the shopfront, the shopping bags, a belt buckle, a door, a wall, a plan, a frame, the hardware on a garment. **It is not restricted and it is not a rank.** Anyone who owns a Valder object owns the mark that is on it, so the V turns up wherever his products turn up, including on what people are wearing and carrying. What it is NOT is a membership pin handed out to insiders — nobody is admitted or refused because of it.

**THE CHROME STARBURST BELONGS TO VALDER HIMSELF AND TO HIS STAFF.** Valder wears an enormous chrome starburst at his throat; his guards carry a small chrome starburst on their cap badges. Customers do not wear it. The founder does not wear his own product mark and his customers do not wear his personal emblem — that separation is deliberate and it is part of the joke.
