WHAT THIS SCENE IS ABOUT — READ THIS, IT DECIDES EVERY CHOICE
================================================================

The family leaves the old house and arrives at the Valder street. They have paid everything they had for this.

**THE MOTHER LEADS.** She walks in front of the others, carrying nothing. The father follows. This is the SAME formation she used in Scene 1, where she pulled the whole family across the concourse as a chain — and the repetition is deliberate. She has been running this family from the first frame of the film, and here she is doing it again, toward a bigger prize. The only difference is that this time she is not holding anyone's hand: she does not need to pull them any more, because now they follow.

THE IMAGE THE WHOLE SCENE EXISTS FOR: **EVERY HOUSE ON THIS STREET IS THE SAME SHAPE AND DIFFERS ONLY IN COLOUR, AND EVERY ONE HAS THE SAME GOLD V ON IT.** Everyone here bought the identical object. The only choice available was the colour. Nobody in the frame finds this strange.

They perform the admiration ceremony again, outdoors, in the same order as they did in front of the gold frame — father nods once, son puffs his chest, grandmother claps three times. The repetition is the joke. It is a ritual now.

THE DAUGHTER BREAKS IT AGAIN. She walks up and PRESSES HER FLAT PALM AGAINST THE WALL, checking whether it is real, and asks a question. Nobody answers her. Second of four.

AND THE LAST BEAT: while everyone else admires their own house, **THE MOTHER IS LOOKING AT THE NEIGHBOURING HOUSES.** Already. On the first day. That single look is what sets the rest of the film in motion.

NOBODY IS UNHAPPY IN THIS SCENE. This is the family's triumph and they are performing it sincerely.

WHAT MARKS A PERSON AS ONE OF THEM — READ THIS, IT REPLACES AN EARLIER RULE
================================================================

**THE GARMENT IS WHAT READS ON SIGHT. THE GOLD V IS A TRADEMARK, AND ANYONE MAY HAVE ONE.**

A Valder coat is unmistakable on sight — floor-length or near it, built from large flat panels of several intensely saturated colours meeting at hard edges, deliberately asymmetric, worn with matching coloured gloves and matching coloured shoes and a scarf at the throat. **Wearing one is what says you belong**, because it is instantly legible across a street. The trademark is not doing that job; the coat is.

**THE GOLD V IS VALDER'S TRADEMARK.** It is stamped, cast, embossed or printed on everything he makes and sells — the shopfront, the shopping bags, a belt buckle, a door, a wall, a plan, a frame, the hardware on a garment. **It is not restricted and it is not a rank.** Anyone who owns a Valder object owns the mark that is on it, so the V turns up wherever his products turn up, including on what people are wearing and carrying. What it is NOT is a membership pin handed out to insiders — nobody is admitted or refused because of it.

**THE CHROME STARBURST BELONGS TO VALDER HIMSELF AND TO HIS STAFF.** Valder wears an enormous chrome starburst at his throat; his guards carry a small chrome starburst on their cap badges. Customers do not wear it. The founder does not wear his own product mark and his customers do not wear his personal emblem — that separation is deliberate and it is part of the joke.
