================================================================

**THE HOUSE IS NOT BROKEN. NOTHING IN IT IS FAULTY. NOTHING IS FALLING APART.**

Every single thing in this house WORKS. The door closes. The lights work. The fittings function. Valder did not cheat anyone and there is nothing here that could be complained about or repaired.

What the house is, is **STRANGE** — strange in the way that expensive things are strange to people who have just become able to afford them. The ceiling comes down low over one end of the table. The door needs a particular technique. One room has no light fitting because the design did not want one there. A fixture is mounted at an unexpected height.

**AND THE FAMILY ADAPTS TO ALL OF IT, PROUDLY AND WITHOUT COMPLAINT — BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT RICH PEOPLE DO.** Rich people adjust themselves to their objects. That is the price of admission and everyone on this street has already paid it. The family is not suffering here. THEY ARE SUCCEEDING. They have learned the house, and they are pleased with themselves for learning it fast.

**THE MOTHER IS THE TEACHER.** She has worked out the door. She demonstrates it. She shows her son the technique and gives him a small nod of approval when he gets it right. This is a scene of a family being coached into a new class, and it is going well.

**DIRECTION FOR PERFORMANCE — THIS IS THE WHOLE SCENE:** nobody sighs. Nobody grimaces. Nobody struggles. Nobody looks tired or defeated. Every awkward posture is performed SMOOTHLY AND WITH PRACTISED EASE, as though it were completely normal, because to them it now is. **If anyone in this scene looks like they are enduring something, the scene is wrong.**
