WHAT THIS SCENE IS — READ THIS, IT DECIDES EVERY CHOICE
================================================================

A man sits at a drafting desk in a huge red hall and draws with his eyes shut, and a room full of people watches him do it.

That is the entire event. Nothing else happens. Nobody arrives, nobody leaves, nothing is announced, nothing falls over. **The scene's job is to make the audience watch someone work for twenty uninterrupted seconds and slowly realise he is not looking at what he is making.**

His hand moves steadily and confidently across the sheet the whole time. The line he draws is CRUDE — plain, blunt, childlike. He is not struggling and he is not in a trance; he simply does not need to look, because what is on the paper was never the point.

**WHAT IS ON THE PAPER IS NEVER SHOWN TO THE CAMERA.** We see his hand, the pencil, the edge of the sheet, the movement — never the drawing's face. Keep the sheet angled away or below the frame line throughout.

================================================================
⚠️ THE CROWD MUST NOT BE ORDERLY — THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT INSTRUCTION HERE
================================================================

**THE PEOPLE IN THIS ROOM ARE NOT ARRANGED. THEY ARE NOT IN ROWS, NOT IN LINES, NOT IN A SEMICIRCLE, NOT IN A BLOC, AND NOT ALL FACING THE SAME WAY.**

They stand the way people actually stand in a room they have wandered into: **scattered at random depths and random distances, in loose ones and twos and threes, at every angle.** Some face Valder. Some face each other. Some have half turned away. Some are talking quietly to the person next to them and not watching him at all. There are gaps between them of uneven size. One stands much closer than everyone else for no reason. Two stand off on their own at the far side.

**THIS IS CRITICAL AND NOT A STYLE PREFERENCE.** A crowd standing in tidy rows all facing one man reads instantly as a political rally, a religious congregation or a cult. This festival bans political statements, and a shot that reads that way is unusable no matter how beautiful it is. The looseness is what keeps it a room of shoppers and journalists looking at a designer.

**NOBODY IS REVERENT.** Nobody has their hands clasped in devotion, nobody bows, nobody gazes worshipfully, nobody raises a hand, nobody holds anything up. They are curious and mildly bored, the way people are at a shop opening.

**THE GUARDS ARE STANDING TOO** — and they are standing the same scattered way, not in a cordon and not in a line. **THERE IS NO SECURITY LINE, NO ROPE, NO BARRIER, NO CORDON HOLDING ANYONE BACK.** A guard here, a guard there, one near the window, one much further back among the onlookers. They are simply present, hands at their sides, not managing anyone.

================================================================
THE PHOTOGRAPHERS
================================================================

Several press photographers are in the room among the others, also scattered, also not in a line. They are working: one is looking down into a camera, one is winding something, one is fitting a fresh bulb into a flash bracket, one is just standing holding his camera at his side doing nothing.

**A FEW FLASHBULBS FIRE DURING THE TWENTY SECONDS — NOT MANY, AND NOT TOGETHER.** Two or three, spaced irregularly, from different positions in the room. Each is a single hard white pop that lights the hall for an instant and is gone.

**VALDER DOES NOT REACT TO ANY OF THEM.** Not a flinch, not a pause, not a tightening of the eyes. His hand keeps moving at exactly the same speed. That non-reaction is the point of including the flashes at all.

People pass through the foreground as the camera advances, occasionally blocking him for a moment and then clearing. That is expected and good — it is a room with people in it, not a corridor.

STYLE
================================================================

ERA: retrofuturist — the future as people in the past imagined it. Streamlined curves, cantilevers that should not hold, moulded plastic, brushed chrome, terrazzo, anodised aluminium, smoked glass, kidney and boomerang shapes, starburst and atom motifs. Technology is big, physical and mechanical — dials, chrome levers, cloth-covered cable, enormous press cameras with separate flash units. **NOTHING IS DIGITAL:** no screens, no flat panels, no LEDs, no digital numerals, no modern devices. An invented place — no country and no year is identifiable.

COLOUR IS LOUD. Every colour in this hall is at the very top of its saturation range, poster ink at full density. Never pastel, never chalky, never washed out.

LIGHTING IS FLAT, EVEN AND FRONTAL, like a printed catalogue page — minimal shadow, everything legible — and the flat light never washes the colour out. The flashbulbs are the only exception.

PHOTOGRAPHED, NOT RENDERED: fine film grain, halation around the brightest highlights, a faint gate weave, softness and slight colour fringing at the edges of the lens. The grain must NOT desaturate the colour. NO HDR, NO digital gloss, NO over-sharpening, NO CGI look.

SKIN IS REAL: visible pores, fine facial hair, uneven tone, small blemishes, crow's feet, knuckle wrinkles. Eyes wet and slightly reflective with fine veins — where any are open. Lips dry, never glossy. Hair with flyaway strands. NOBODY IS AIRBRUSHED OR BEAUTIFIED.

PALE means ORDINARY INDOOR HUMAN PALLOR — the look of someone who has not been outdoors in years. **IT IS NEVER GREEN, NEVER GREY, NEVER BLUE, NEVER SILVER.** Think of a clerk at the end of a long winter — NOT a creature, NOT a corpse, NOT anything from science fiction. If any face reads as alien, undead or inhuman, it is wrong.

NOBODY LOOKS AT THE CAMERA.

NOBODY SPEAKS AN AUDIBLE WORD IN THIS SCENE.
