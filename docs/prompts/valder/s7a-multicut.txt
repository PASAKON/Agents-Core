WHAT THIS SCENE IS ABOUT — READ THIS, IT DECIDES EVERY CHOICE
================================================================

This is the end of the film. The family is poor again.

They are sitting on the floor of the most expensive house any of them has ever lived in, eating a very small amount of food, because there is no furniture left to sit on. Everything has been sold.

TWO THINGS SURVIVED THE SELLING, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT:
1. The gold frame on the wall, with the crumpled drawing in it.
2. The parents' clothes, which are immaculate, saturated and expensive.

The people who own nothing are still perfectly dressed, sitting on a bare floor, under a piece of rubbish in a gold frame. Nobody comments on this. Nobody is ashamed. THE PARENTS DO NOT LOOK UNHAPPY — they look composed, exactly as they have all film.

THE LAST SPOKEN LINE OF THE FILM belongs to the daughter, and it is the obvious practical suggestion that nobody will ever act on:

"Can we sell that one?"

She means the frame. NOBODY ANSWERS HER. Nobody even looks up. That is the fourth and final time in this film that she asks a question and is not answered, and this one is the answer to the whole story: they will starve before they sell the thing that makes them who they are.

DO NOT MAKE THIS SAD-LOOKING. No tears, no huddling, no dramatic lighting, no comforting. Everyone sits upright and eats politely off a bare floor. The flatness is what makes it land.

WHAT MARKS A PERSON AS ONE OF THEM — READ THIS, IT REPLACES AN EARLIER RULE
================================================================

**THE GARMENT IS WHAT READS ON SIGHT. THE GOLD V IS A TRADEMARK, AND ANYONE MAY HAVE ONE.**

A Valder coat is unmistakable on sight — floor-length or near it, built from large flat panels of several intensely saturated colours meeting at hard edges, deliberately asymmetric, worn with matching coloured gloves and matching coloured shoes and a scarf at the throat. **Wearing one is what says you belong**, because it is instantly legible across a street. The trademark is not doing that job; the coat is.

**THE GOLD V IS VALDER'S TRADEMARK.** It is stamped, cast, embossed or printed on everything he makes and sells — the shopfront, the shopping bags, a belt buckle, a door, a wall, a plan, a frame, the hardware on a garment. **It is not restricted and it is not a rank.** Anyone who owns a Valder object owns the mark that is on it, so the V turns up wherever his products turn up, including on what people are wearing and carrying. What it is NOT is a membership pin handed out to insiders — nobody is admitted or refused because of it.

**THE CHROME STARBURST BELONGS TO VALDER HIMSELF AND TO HIS STAFF.** Valder wears an enormous chrome starburst at his throat; his guards carry a small chrome starburst on their cap badges. Customers do not wear it. The founder does not wear his own product mark and his customers do not wear his personal emblem — that separation is deliberate and it is part of the joke.


================================================================
WHERE THE GOLD V APPEARS — IT IS ON EVERYTHING VALDER MAKES
================================================================

**EVERY SINGLE THING VALDER DESIGNS, MAKES OR SELLS CARRIES HIS GOLD V, AND SO DOES EVERY BUILDING HIS COMPANY OWNS.** It is his trademark and it is applied without exception.

On PRODUCTS: garments carry it at the chest or on the belt hardware. Shopping bags carry it large and centred. Furniture carries it on a leg or an underside. A plan carries it in the signature. A frame carries it on the mount.

On ARCHITECTURE: **every house he designed has one set into its facade** — the same V, the same size relative to the wall, on every house in a row of them. His shopfront carries an enormous one. His studio and offices carry one. A door he designed carries one on its hardware.

**IT IS ALWAYS THE SAME SHAPE:** a capital V in brushed matte warm gold, flat, with thick strokes WIDE AT THE TOP tapering to a sharp point at the bottom — no bevel, no crossbar, no serif, no 3D extrusion, no outline. It scales but never redraws.

**WHAT THE MARK ACTUALLY SAYS ABOUT THE PERSON CARRYING IT.** Anyone may own it, because anyone may buy his products — it is not a rank and nobody is admitted or refused because of it. But everyone in this world reads it the same way: **it means money was not a problem.** Carrying the V says you bought this without it costing you anything you needed. That is why people want it on them and want it seen.

**AND THAT IS THE WHOLE JOKE OF THIS FILM.** This family wears and carries the mark that means money is not a problem, during exactly the period when money becomes the only problem they have. The signal and the truth run in opposite directions, and nobody in the frame ever mentions it.
