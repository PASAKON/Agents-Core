WHAT THIS SCENE IS ABOUT — READ THIS, IT DECIDES EVERY CHOICE
================================================================

**THIS IS A SCENE AT THE FAMILY DINNER TABLE.** It is the evening the boy brought the crumpled drawing home. It has already been put into a gold frame, and the frame is **STANDING UPRIGHT IN THE MIDDLE OF THE DINNER TABLE, FACING OUT INTO THE ROOM, WHERE A CENTREPIECE WOULD GO.** The family is eating around it. Nobody has hung it on a wall yet — tonight it is a guest at the meal.

EVERYONE CAN SEE THAT THE DRAWING IS CRUDE. That is not in doubt and nobody is fooled. What it has is the signature and the gold V worked into it, and that is enough for all of them.

**EACH PERSON AT THIS TABLE FEELS A DIFFERENT AMOUNT, AND THE EXACT CALIBRATION IS THE SCENE. GET THIS RIGHT AND NOTHING ELSE MATTERS:**

- **THE MOTHER — the happiest person at the table.** This is the best night of her life and she is not hiding it, though she expresses it in a contained, upright, formal way: very straight back, hands arranging things that do not need arranging, eyes returning to the frame again and again. **SHE IS THE ENGINE OF THIS SCENE AND OF THIS FILM.** She put it in the frame. She stood it on the table. Everything the others do, they do after her and because of her.

- **THE GRANDMOTHER — equally happy, and the only one who shows it openly.** Her joy is completely genuine and slightly overwhelming her. Her eyes shine wet. She claps.

- **THE FATHER — pleased because his wife is pleased, and for no other reason.** He goes along with it. He nods, he agrees, he plays his part — but **HE IS WATCHING HER MORE THAN HE IS WATCHING THE FRAME.** His pleasure is second-hand and it should read that way.

- **THE SON — PROUD.** He is the one who picked it up. He brought this home. Tonight, for the first time in this film, HIS PARENTS ARE HAPPY AND IT IS BECAUSE OF HIM. He sits up straighter than he ever has. He does not speak, he does not boast, he just glows quietly. **This is the payoff of the previous scene, where nobody in the world could see him at all — and it should ache slightly, because the only way he became visible was by bringing his family an object.**

- **THE DAUGHTER — completely unmoved.** Not sad, not sulking, not disapproving. Just **INDIFFERENT.** She eats. She is the only person at the table who is treating this like an ordinary dinner. When she finally does look at the frame, she looks at it properly, the way a person looks at a thing they are trying to understand — and then she says the one line, and NOBODY ANSWERS HER AND NOBODY TURNS THEIR HEAD. First of four times in the film.

NOBODY IS BEING STUPID AND NOBODY IS A FOOL. They are sincerely happy about a thing that is worthless, and the comedy lives in the gap between the size of the feeling and the size of the object.
