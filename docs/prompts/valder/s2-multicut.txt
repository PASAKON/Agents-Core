================================================================
WHAT THIS SCENE IS ABOUT — READ THIS, IT DECIDES EVERY CHOICE
================================================================

The family has framed the crumpled drawing in gold and hung it in the middle of their cramped, faded living room. Now they take turns admiring it, in a fixed order, like a ceremony.

**THE MOTHER IS THE ENGINE OF THIS SCENE AND OF THIS FILM.** She hung it. She positions the family. She gives each person their turn with a small movement of her head. The father is the one who nods, but she is the one who decides. Watch for this in every shot: SHE ACTS FIRST, THE OTHERS FOLLOW.

EVERYONE CAN SEE THAT THE DRAWING IS CRUDE. That is not in doubt and nobody is fooled. What it has is the signature, and the gold V worked into it, and that is enough for all of them. This is the film's thesis stated in its simplest form.

THE DAUGHTER BREAKS THE CEREMONY. She walks past the line everyone else stops at, gets close enough to touch the glass, tilts her head, and ACTUALLY LOOKS AT IT rather than admiring it. Then she turns and asks the adults a question. NOBODY ANSWERS HER AND NOBODY EVEN TURNS THEIR HEAD. This is the first of four times in the film that she asks and is ignored.

NOBODY IS BEING STUPID AND NOBODY IS A FOOL. They are performing a ritual that they believe in completely and sincerely. The comedy lives in the gap between the ceremony and the object it is being performed for.

================================================================
POOR IS NOT DIRTY — THIS IS THE MOST MISREAD INSTRUCTION IN THIS FILM
================================================================

This family is not wealthy, but they are not shabby and they are NOT DIRTY. Clothes clean, pressed, neatly mended. Hair combed. Faces washed. Shoes old but polished. The furniture is old and mismatched but DUSTED AND CARED FOR. Poverty reads through the AGE of things and through visible careful repairs — never through stains, grime, grease, dust, tears or mess.

NO dirt. NO stains. NO grime. NO smudges. NO greasy hair. NO torn fabric. NO dust. NO squalor.

Everyone in this scene has WARM skin — healthy, slightly ruddy, outdoor. Nobody has crossed over yet. This is the family at their most alive, and the audience should be able to feel the difference later when the parents go pale.

Everyone must read as REAL PHOTOGRAPHED HUMAN BEINGS with visible pores and real skin texture. NO plastic skin, NO airbrushing.

================================================================
STYLE — APPLIES TO EVERY SHOT
================================================================

ERA: a retrofuturist world — the future as people in the past imagined it. Swept aerodynamic curves, moulded plastic, chromium, formica, starburst motifs, boomerang shapes, tapered splayed legs, a valve radio in a wooden cabinet. THERE IS NOTHING DIGITAL ANYWHERE. No screens, no LEDs, no digital numerals, no cables. An invented place — no country and no year is identifiable.

COLOUR AND CLUTTER ARE THE CLASS SYSTEM: wealth is DEEP SATURATED COLOUR IN LARGE FLAT PLANES and EMPTY SPACE. Poverty is the SAME HUES BUT PALE AND WASHED OUT, and space CRAMMED FULL. This room is the film's clearest statement of the poor half of that system — and the gold frame in the middle of it is the only saturated thing in the shot.

THE CAMERA IS ALWAYS LEVEL. No dutch angles, no tilting, ever.

LIGHTING IS FLAT AND FRONTAL, like a property brochure — minimal shadow, everything legible, objects square to camera.

PHOTOGRAPHED, NOT RENDERED: fine film grain, halation around bright highlights, faint gate weave, softness and slight colour fringing at the edges of the lens. The grain must NOT desaturate the colour. NO HDR, NO digital glossiness, NO CGI sheen.

PERFORMANCE IS FLAT AND CEREMONIAL: nobody smiles broadly, nobody laughs, nobody grimaces. Movements precise, slow and formal, as though rehearsed. Total sincerity delivered completely deadpan. Characters take their turns IN ORDER.

NOBODY LOOKS AT THE CAMERA. Every gaze is aimed at a person or an object inside the frame.

ALL SPOKEN DIALOGUE IN THIS FILM IS IN ENGLISH.
