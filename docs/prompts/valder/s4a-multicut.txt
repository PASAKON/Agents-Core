WHO AND WHAT EACH REFERENCE IS. READ THIS BEFORE ANY SHOT BELOW.
================================================================

@project_valder_char_mother — THE MOTHER. A woman in her forties. She stands very straight, hands habitually clasped in front of her. Her clothes are her own best: a plain dress she altered herself, and if you look closely the seams have been sewn twice. The colours are PALE — washed out, thin, the same hues everyone else wears but drained. Her clothes are CLEAN, PRESSED and CAREFULLY MENDED. Her skin is WARM and slightly ruddy — she is not one of the pale people yet. SHE IS ONE PERSON. She appears in every shot of this film scene.

@project_valder_char_neighbor — THE NEIGHBOUR. A woman in her forties who lives on this street and has lived the wealthy life for years. She wears TWO OR THREE deeply saturated colours in large flat blocks, worn with complete confidence. Her face is composed, pleasant, and completely closed — she is not sneering and not cruel, she is simply not interested. Her skin is PALE in the ordinary indoor human way. SHE IS ONE PERSON. She is never seen fully — only a vertical slice of her, through a door that never opens more than a hand's width.

@project_valder_loc_street_row — THE STREET. A row of houses that are all EXACTLY THE SAME SHAPE and differ only in colour. Swept rooflines, cantilevered carports, huge empty lawns mown flat. Deeply saturated colour on every facade. No cars. No people except the ones this scene puts there.

@project_valder_loc_neighbor_door — THE NEIGHBOUR'S FRONT DOOR. A single door set into an enormous flat plane of deeply saturated colour, with almost nothing else on the wall. Chromium door furniture. Immaculate. It is the most expensive-looking empty space imaginable.

@project_valder_prop_mark — THE GOLD V. Valder's maker's mark: a capital letter V in polished warm gold, thick even strokes, tapered and narrowing to a sharp point at the bottom. It is stamped into everything Valder designs and worn by everyone who belongs. IT IS THE SAME SHAPE AT EVERY SIZE — no simplified version exists.

THERE IS EXACTLY ONE MOTHER AND EXACTLY ONE NEIGHBOUR IN THIS ENTIRE FILM SCENE. Never two of either, never a copy, never a double in the background, never a reflection that produces a second person. NO DUPLICATE CHARACTER.

================================================================
WHAT THIS SCENE IS ABOUT — READ THIS, IT DECIDES EVERY CHOICE
================================================================

The mother has just moved onto this street. She has paid everything her family had to be here. She has put on the best clothes she owns and she is bringing a gift to a neighbour, the way a person does.

She is turned away at the door, and the reason is visible on screen: THE NEIGHBOUR IS WEARING THE GOLD V AND THE MOTHER IS NOT. Owning the house was not enough. There is a second thing you have to buy, and she has just found out it exists.

NOBODY IS CRUEL IN THIS SCENE. The neighbour is polite. The door closes gently. There is no argument, no raised voice, no slammed door. The whole injury is delivered through a pair of eyes travelling down a dress and back up again, and through a door that opens a hand's width and no further. That restraint IS the scene. If anyone in this shot looks angry or contemptuous, it is wrong.

================================================================
POOR IS NOT DIRTY — THIS IS THE MOST MISREAD INSTRUCTION IN THIS FILM
================================================================

The mother is not wealthy, but she is not shabby and she is NOT DIRTY. Her clothes are clean, pressed, and neatly mended. Her hair is combed. Her face is washed. Her shoes are old but polished. Her poverty reads through the AGE of her things and through visible, careful repairs — a seam sewn twice, a cuff turned, a button that does not quite match. It NEVER reads through stains, grime, grease, dust, tears, or messiness. She is a person who has taken enormous trouble over her appearance today. That effort must be visible, and it must be visibly not enough.

NO dirt. NO stains. NO grime. NO smudges. NO greasy hair. NO torn fabric. NO dust. NO mud. NO sweat. NO unwashed skin.

================================================================
WHAT PALE MEANS HERE
================================================================

The neighbour's skin is PALE — but pale means ORDINARY HUMAN INDOOR PALLOR. It is the skin of a person who spends their life inside and does not see the sun. IT IS NEVER GREEN, NEVER GREY, NEVER BLUE, NEVER SILVER. Her skin has a normal warm undertone, visible pores, and real human texture. Think of a clerk at the end of a long winter, or someone who has been in an office for twenty years. NOT a creature, NOT a corpse, NOT anything from science fiction, NOT anything from a horror film.

The mother's skin is WARM — a healthy, slightly ruddy, outdoor tone. The DIFFERENCE between the two women's skin is one of the things the audience should feel without being told.

Both women must read as REAL PHOTOGRAPHED HUMAN BEINGS.

================================================================
STYLE — APPLIES TO EVERY SHOT
================================================================

ERA: a retrofuturist world — the future as people in the past imagined it. Swept aerodynamic curves, cantilevers, moulded plastic, chromium, terrazzo, formica, starburst motifs, boomerang shapes, tapered splayed legs. THERE IS NOTHING DIGITAL ANYWHERE. No screens, no LEDs, no digital numerals, no cables. It is an invented place — no country and no year is identifiable.

COLOUR IS THE CLASS SYSTEM: wealth is DEEP SATURATED COLOUR IN LARGE FLAT PLANES, and emptiness. Poverty is the SAME HUES BUT PALE AND WASHED OUT. The neighbour's door and wall are drenched in colour. The mother is the palest thing in every frame she is in.

THE CAMERA IS ALWAYS LEVEL. No dutch angles, no tilting, ever.

LIGHTING IS FLAT AND FRONTAL, like a property brochure — minimal shadow, everything legible, objects square to camera.

PHOTOGRAPHED, NOT RENDERED: fine film grain, halation around bright highlights, a faint gate weave, softness and slight colour fringing at the edges of the lens. The grain must NOT desaturate the colour. NO HDR, NO digital glossiness, NO CGI sheen.

PERFORMANCE IS FLAT AND CEREMONIAL: nobody smiles broadly, nobody laughs, nobody grimaces. Movements are precise, slow and formal, as though rehearsed. Total sincerity delivered completely deadpan.

NOBODY LOOKS AT THE CAMERA. Every gaze is aimed at a person or an object inside the frame.

ALL SPOKEN DIALOGUE IN THIS FILM IS IN ENGLISH.

================================================================
WHAT MARKS A PERSON AS ONE OF THEM — READ THIS, IT REPLACES AN EARLIER RULE
================================================================

**THE GARMENT IS THE BADGE. NOBODY IN THIS FILM WEARS A LAPEL PIN OR A BROOCH BEARING THE GOLD V. NOT ONE PERSON, IN ANY SCENE.**

A Valder coat is unmistakable on sight — floor-length or near it, built from large flat panels of several intensely saturated colours meeting at hard edges, deliberately asymmetric, worn with matching coloured gloves and matching coloured shoes and a scarf at the throat. **Wearing one is what says you belong.** That is the whole signalling system and it needs no extra emblem.

**THE GOLD V IS A MAKER'S MARK ON OBJECTS, NOT A BADGE ON PEOPLE.** It appears stamped, cast, embossed or printed on things Valder made and sold: the shopfront, the shopping bags, a belt buckle, a door, a wall, a plan, a frame. It never appears pinned to a chest.

**THE CHROME STARBURST BELONGS TO VALDER HIMSELF AND TO HIS STAFF.** Valder wears an enormous chrome starburst at his throat; his guards carry a small chrome starburst on their cap badges. Customers do not wear it. The founder does not wear his own product mark and his customers do not wear his personal emblem — that separation is deliberate and it is part of the joke.

=== SHOT 1 — 0:00 to 0:03 — THE WALK UP ===

Wide. @project_valder_loc_street_row. Camera at chest height, locked, does not move.

@project_valder_char_mother walks up a flat empty lawn toward a house, alone, seen from behind and slightly to the side. She carries a small parcel wrapped in plain paper and tied with string, held in both hands in front of her.

The street around her is drenched in saturated colour — every facade a different intense hue, all the houses exactly the same shape. She is the only pale thing in the frame and she is very small in it.

She walks the way a person walks toward something they have rehearsed.

@project_valder_prop_mark — the gold V — pinned at @project_valder_char_neighbor's collar, catching the light. It fills a large part of the frame. Polished warm gold against deep saturated fabric. It is the brightest object anywhere in this scene.

=== SHOT 5 — 0:12 to 0:15 — THE LOOK, AND THE LINE ===

Medium, favouring @project_valder_char_mother, with the narrow gap and the slice of @project_valder_char_neighbor at the edge of frame. Camera locked.

THE NEIGHBOUR'S EYES TRAVEL SLOWLY DOWN THE MOTHER'S CLOTHES AND BACK UP AGAIN. This is a deliberate, unhurried movement, and it is the only thing that happens in the shot. Her face stays completely pleasant throughout. She is not sneering. She is pricing.

"Oh. You're the new ones."

@project_valder_char_mother does not reply. Her expression does not change either. She is still holding the parcel out.

=== SHOT 6 — 0:15 to 0:18 — THE DOOR CLOSES ===

Medium. Camera at chest height, locked, same position as shot 3.

The door closes. GENTLY AND COMPLETELY — it is not slammed, there is no anger in it. The narrow gap narrows to nothing. The latch engages with a single small precise click.

What is left in the frame is an enormous flat plane of deep saturated colour with a closed door in it, and @project_valder_char_mother standing in front of it, still holding the parcel in both hands.

=== SHOT 7 — 0:18 to 0:20 — HER OWN COLLAR ===

Close on @project_valder_char_mother, from the front. Camera at chest height, locked.

She stands facing the closed door. She does not knock again. She does not turn to leave.

SHE LOOKS DOWN AT HER OWN COLLAR. Her chin drops slightly and her eyes go to her own lapel — the same place where the gold V sat on the other woman.

THERE IS NOTHING THERE. Just pale, clean, carefully mended fabric.

Hold on that. The film scene ends with her looking at the empty place on her own clothes.
