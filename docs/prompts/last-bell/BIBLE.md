# THE LAST BELL (ระฆังสุดท้ายแห่งนครน้ำ) — story bible

An epic Thai fable, English dialogue, about 8 minutes. Engine: Seedance 2.0 on champa.io, 16:9.
Status: plot draft v1 (2026-10-03), not yet a locked script. CEO's words: NOTES.md.

## 1 · The spine (approved by the CEO, 2026-10-03)

1. **Goal.** The floating city of Suwannawari will be swallowed by the storm of a hundred years. Legend says
   the giant bell atop the great chedi wakes the guardian Naga, who holds back the sea. Kaew, a young temple
   bell-ringer, must ring it before the wave arrives.
2. **Obstacle.** The bridge to the chedi collapses in the storm, the bell rope snaps, thousands of people flee
   the other way, and a wave as tall as a mountain rises on the horizon.
3. **Tactic.** Kaew crosses the wreckage, climbs the chedi through the gale, and strikes the bell until her
   hands bleed. Its sound fills the sky.
4. **Reversal.** The Naga wakes, but the bell was never a call. It was the chain that kept the Naga asleep for a
   hundred years, and the storm was the Naga all along. Kaew has set it free with her own hands.
5. **Value shift.** Kaew does not run. She taps the cracked bell in the rhythm of her grandmother's lullaby.
   The Naga stops in front of her, then coils around the city as a living wall against the wave. At dawn the
   city stands, the bell is broken, and Kaew is the Naga's new keeper.

Audience verdict on Kaew: brave child → the one who doomed the city → the only one who can speak to the Naga.
On the Naga: guardian → monster → the city's wall.

## 1b · Setup: what the viewer knows before the storm (CEO 2026-10-04)

The CEO found act A "จู่ๆ ก็เข้าเรื่องเลย": nobody knew who Kaew is, what she is to Yai, what the city is, why the
Governor acts as he does, or why only Kaew can ring the great bell. Backstory he chose (all four picks the same night):

- **Kaew** is Yai Bua's orphaned granddaughter. Her mother was the city's bell-keeper and is dead. Yai kept the bells
  before her and still owns the keeper's red-headed mallet, but her legs can no longer climb. Kaew rings the morning
  bell every day: she is the last keeper (the title).
- **Why only Kaew:** only a keeper may touch the great bell, because only a keeper knows its one song. When the storm
  comes, the adults flee on the Governor's barges or onto the roofs, so Kaew is the only keeper left who can climb.
- **The city:** the founders made a pact with the Naga. Every house hangs its own bell and rings it to answer the
  keeper's morning bell, "to keep the sea asleep"; a bell is hung for every newborn. The great bell on the chedi is
  bound in chains and never rung except in the storm of a hundred years.
- **The Governor** owns the long bridge to the chedi island and charges a toll; the poor pay with their family bells,
  which his guard drops into a chest. Cutting that bridge in the storm (B04) is the same man.

Setup shots (round 4, free lane): A00a Yai wakes Kaew ("Up, little keeper. Your mother never once rang late."),
A03c the city answers the morning bell house by house, A09a Mek and Kaew pass the chained great bell ("Only a keeper
may touch it. It has only one song."), A09b the toll gate ("Please, it's my son's birth bell." / "No coin, no
crossing. The Governor's bridge."). Act A order: A01, A00a, A03, A04, A03c, A04b, A05, A05b, A06b, A07, A07b, A08,
A08b, A09a, A09b, then B01.

## 2 · Look

- **Grade (CEO):** surreal yellow-green. Acid yellow-green sky, chartreuse highlights, deep olive shadows,
  warm gold practical light (oil lamps, gold leaf), soft haze. Photoreal, cinematic, a fable painted in light.
- The storm pushes the grade darker (bottle-green, black-green clouds, yellow lightning) and never turns blue.
- Dawn at the end returns to pale gold-green.
- One saturated colour per character (CAST.md), so they read apart even small in frame.

## 3 · Thai identity inside an English film

Teak stilt houses with steep gabled roofs and crossed *kalae* finials; canals and long-tail boats; a white-and-gold
chedi; naga stair rails; temple bells and wind chimes everywhere (the "city of bells"); chong kraben, mor hom
indigo, pha khao ma cloth; betel; jasmine garlands; an original Thai lullaby, sung in Thai, which is the key to the
ending. Names stay Thai: Kaew, Yai Bua, Mek, Phaya Nak.

## 4 · Plot, draft v1 (about 8:00)

Times are targets. Shot counts come later in STORYBOARD.md.

### A · The city of bells (0:00–1:30)
- A1 Dawn over Suwannawari in yellow-green mist. Thousands of small bells chime in the wind.
- A2 Kaew runs across footbridges with her red-thread mallet, climbs a small bell tower, and rings the morning
  bell. The city wakes.
- A3 Yai Bua's house. Yai teaches Kaew the lullaby rhythm on the little bells: three slow, one fast.
  YAI: "Every bell in this city has a voice. The great one has only one song."
  KAEW: "Which song?" Yai smiles and hums the first line in Thai.
- A4 Mek's long-tail boat. Mek teases Kaew. The lagoon turns yellow, and fish leap and flee out to sea.

### B · The storm and the Governor (1:30–3:00)
- B1 The Governor on his golden barge: "The storm of a hundred years is coming. My barges leave at dusk, for
  those who can pay." MEK: "And the rest of us?"
- B2 The old people of the city repeat the legend: ring the great bell and the Naga will rise to hold back the
  sea. Kaew listens.
- B3 The sky turns black-green. The Governor's barges leave, and his men cut the ropes of the long bridge to the
  chedi island so the crowd cannot follow.
- B4 Yai, too weak to climb, gives Kaew the red-thread mallet. YAI: "If you ring it, ring it right."

### C · The crossing (3:00–5:00)
- C1 Mek rows Kaew through flooded canals. Houses fold into the water. People cling to rooftops.
- C2 At the broken bridge the boat is smashed. Kaew climbs the floating wreckage to the island.
- C3 She climbs the naga stair rails of the chedi in the gale. Mek holds the line below.
- C4 At the top the bell rope is gone. On the horizon, a wave as tall as a mountain.
- C5 Kaew strikes the bell with the mallet, then with her bare hands. The sound shakes the sky.

### D · The chain (5:00–6:15)
- D1 Silence. The sea bulges. The Naga rises, colossal, jade and gold. The people on the roofs cheer.
- D2 The Naga roars. The storm doubles. Its tail capsizes the Governor's golden barge.
- D3 Kaew sees the engraving on the bell: a naga bound in chains. KAEW: "No… I woke it." The bell cracks.

### E · The lullaby (6:15–8:00)
- E1 The Naga's head descends to the top of the chedi, eye to eye with Kaew. MEK, from below: "Kaew, run!"
- E2 A flash of memory: Yai singing. "The great one has only one song."
- E3 Kaew taps the cracked bell, three slow, one fast, and sings the lullaby in Thai.
- E4 The Naga stills, listens, closes its eyes. Then it coils around the whole city and rises as a living wall.
  The great wave breaks on its body.
- E5 Dawn, pale gold-green. The city stands. The Naga sleeps coiled around the island, its head beside the
  chedi. The bell lies broken. Kaew sits by the Naga's head with the mallet: the new keeper. Mek arrives by boat.
  Yai's little bells chime. Title: THE LAST BELL.

## 5 · The lullaby (draft, original lyric — CEO to approve)

> นอนเถิดนาคา ใต้ฟ้าสีทอง
> ระฆังจะร้อง กล่อมเจ้าหลับไป
> สามช้าหนึ่งเร็ว ดังลมพัดใบ
> นอนเถิดนะใจ นครยังรอ

(English subtitle: "Sleep now, great Naga, under the golden sky. The bell will sing you to sleep. Three slow, one
fast, like the wind in the leaves. Sleep now, the city still waits.")
