The painting is hanging crooked. He tries to straighten it. That is what brings

catastrophe starts with a man tidying something because it was slightly wrong.

---------------------------------------------------------------

**90 cm wide × 70 cm tall, landscape, thin dark frame about 4 cm deep.**

That is: a little wider than Dupe's shoulders, light enough for one man to lift
alone, small enough to ride on the cart without overhanging it. State the
proportion explicitly in the prompt — roughly 9:7, clearly wider than tall —

THE CRACK — MATCH THE REFERENCE, NEVER REINVENT IT
---------------------------------------------------------------
⚠️ **Bind the existing crack plate and match it exactly.** This is the same fault
that broke the wall-POV shots: left to itself the model draws a different crack
every time, and the audience reads a different object each time. It is ONE crack
and it must be identical everywhere it appears — here, in the wall-POV frames,
and at the end of the film.

Same length, same jagged path, same branch points, same chipped plaster at the
edges. Do not add fractures. Do not make it a spider-web or a shattered-glass
pattern. **One crack, roughly a foot long, exactly as the reference draws it.**

---------------------------------------------------------------
THE SCENE
---------------------------------------------------------------
The gallery, EARLY MORNING BEFORE OPENING. Empty of people. Warm orange cove
light already on. Terracotta terrazzo floor.

**THE PAINTING HANGS VISIBLY CROOKED** on the hero wall — tilted several degrees,
enough that it reads as wrong the instant you see it. The small brass plaque sits
low beneath it: THE ABSENCE OF MEANING / Valder / $2,000,000.

DUPE comes along the wall with his cart, working, unhurried, quietly content.
Indian man in his early thirties, short, white uniform with orange trim and a
matching cap, gold V on cap and chest.

**HE NOTICES THE TILT.** He stops. He looks at it the way a person looks at a
frame that is off by two degrees.

He reaches up with both hands and **gently tries to straighten it** — a small,
careful, well-meant adjustment. Nothing forceful.

**IT COMES OFF ITS FIXING.** It drops, strikes the floor, and ends leaning
against the wall on its bottom edge. The frame is not destroyed.

**ONE SOUND: a short dry crack of plaster** as it goes.

He freezes. He looks at the wall.

**THERE IS THE CRACK** — where the painting was, exactly as the reference draws
it, roughly a foot long, plaster chipped a little at the edges.

He looks left. He looks right. Nobody.

He picks the painting up, carries it to his cart, and **slots it upright into the
cart**, face turned outward, where it stays for the rest of the film.

**HOLD ON THE WALL as he leaves frame:** the plaque still in place, and above it
now nothing at all but the crack.

Camera: static and level, or one slow unhurried move. No handheld, no shake.
