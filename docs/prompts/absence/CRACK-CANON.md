# THE CRACK — canonical description, read off the plate by eye (CTO 2026-09-04)

Every scene that shows the hero wall binds `@project_absence_loc_wall_pov_e` for
the crack's shape. Until tonight all four scene files described it as **"about
five long thin clean cracks radiating outward"**. That is wrong, and it had been
copied from file to file since August.

The generated aperture plate came back with four arms and the CEO rejected it
for not matching. Zooming the real plate 7× shows the reference has **four long
arms too** — the plate was right and our text was wrong. What actually does not
match is the CENTRE, and the centre had never been described at all.

This file is what the plate shows. Copy it into any prompt that needs the crack,
and never write "five arms" again.

## The centre is not a hole. It is a solid black blade.

The middle is a **solid black shape like an arrowhead pointing DOWN** — broad
across the top, sides falling inward, tapering to a sharp point at the bottom,
roughly as wide as it is tall. It reads as a piece of wall that has come away
completely, not as a round puncture.

Two features on it are unmistakable and get lost every single time they are not
named explicitly:

- **A HOOKED SPUR curls off its upper right**, like a comma or a claw — a short
  stroke that leaves the body, bends back on itself and stops. It is the most
  identifiable thing about this crack.
- **A NOTCH at the upper left**, where the vertical arm meets the body.

## Four long arms, at these clock positions

Measured from the centre, on a clock face:

1. **12 o'clock**, leaning slightly left as it rises. Long and fine, carrying
   one distinct **oval bead** about a third of the way up where it widens and
   narrows again. Ends in a hairline.
2. **1:30 — the longest and heaviest.** Leaves the top right and climbs toward
   the upper-right corner. Lumpy along its whole length, thickening and thinning
   repeatedly rather than tapering smoothly, and kinking once about halfway.
3. **9:30**, running down and left at a shallow angle. Beaded like the others,
   thinner overall, finishing in a fine point.
4. **4:30**, falling down and right from the bottom point of the blade. Kinks
   noticeably partway down and throws a small **barb** off to one side near its
   end, then tapers to a needle.

Plus the hooked spur above, which sits close enough to the body that it reads as
part of the centre rather than a fifth arm. Four arms and a hook.

## Two qualities every arm must have

- **BEADED, NOT TAPERED.** Each arm swells into small dark lozenges and pinches
  back to a hairline, several times along its length. A smooth even taper is
  wrong and is what the model produces when left alone.
- **A BRIGHT LIP.** Every edge carries a thin highlight where the broken plaster
  turns to the light. The arms are black with a pale rim, never flat black on
  flat wall.

## The paste paragraph

> A star-shaped break whose centre is a SOLID BLACK ARROWHEAD POINTING DOWN —
> broad at the top, tapering to a point at the bottom, with a short HOOKED SPUR
> curling off its upper right like a comma, and a notch at its upper left. FOUR
> long arms leave it and no more: one rising at 12 o'clock with a single oval
> bead a third of the way up; the longest and heaviest climbing toward 1:30,
> lumpy and kinked once; one running down-left at 9:30, thinner, ending in a
> fine point; and one falling down-right at 4:30 that kinks partway and throws a
> small barb near its needle-sharp end. Every arm is BEADED — swelling into
> small dark lozenges and pinching back to a hairline, several times along its
> length, never a smooth taper — and every edge carries a thin bright lip where
> the broken plaster catches the light.
