DH1 – DH4 · THE DOLL'S HOUSE · a second way of seeing the film
===============================================================
CEO 2026-08-29. A new recurring viewpoint, cut in among the existing coverage.

**The idea.** Two or three times the film pulls all the way out and looks DOWN
into the gallery as if into a doll's house: a lit rectangular box floating in
a glossy black void, the people inside reduced to figures on a board. It is
the coldest angle in the film and it says the thing the script never says out
loud — that this is a game being played on a small table, and that the man
cleaning it is a piece like any other.

Use it sparingly. Three or four cuts across ninety minutes of screen time.
Its power comes from how rarely it appears.

REFERENCES — all four, each with a job:
@loc_dollhouse — THE FRAME ITSELF: the gallery as an open rectangular box seen
  from steeply above, floating in glossy black, red door small at the top of
  frame, ONE jagged crack in the near plane. Match it exactly.
@project_absence_char_cleaner_c — Dupe: white uniform, orange trim, cap, gold V.
@prop_cart_b — the cart, the painting standing in it face outward.
(plus the character named in each scene below)

SHARED RULES — every DH shot:
- **The camera NEVER moves.** Locked off, steeply elevated, no drift, no push,
  no rotation. The box is a photograph of an object; only the figures move.
- **Figures are SMALL** — a fifth of the frame height at most. We read them by
  costume colour and posture, never by facial expression. No close-ups, ever.
- **The black void stays clean** on all four sides. Nothing enters it, nobody
  walks out into it, nothing is lit in it.
- **The crack stays in the near plane**, floating in front, unmoved by anything
  happening in the room below.
- Warm shadow, cold white grade; fine film grain, faint gate weave.
- 720p · 16:9 · High · Sound On. Room tone always, plus the one sound named.
