⚠️ CTO NOTE FOR THE CEO — S8 DOES NOT FIT IN ONE CLIP

Valder's five acquisition stories run about 100 spoken words, and the sixth
improvisation another 60. At a natural speaking rate that is roughly 65 seconds
of dialogue. A Seedance clip is 20-25 seconds, which holds about 60 words.

So S8 is written below as THREE clips — S8a, S8b, S8c. No line was cut, no line
was changed. The five stories simply had to be dealt out across two clips, and
the improvisation gets its own, which it should anyway — it is the pivot of the
film and it deserves an unbroken take.

Shot-count consequence: S8 costs 3 clips instead of 1, so 2 takes of it is 6
clips, not 2.

SCENE 7 — VALDER ARRIVES · 20s
===============================================================

VALDER walks into the hall. Tall, very thin, elderly, silver hair swept straight
back, small round tinted sunglasses. A suit jacket panelled in magenta, yellow,
green, blue and red over a scarlet waistcoat, purple trousers, a long mustard
scarf, and two deliberately mismatched shoes — one green, one oxblood.

HE SMILES CONSTANTLY WHILE HE SPEAKS — a warm, charming, faintly ingratiating
smile, the smile of a man who wants to be liked and is extremely good at being
liked. It is charisma, not menace, and it does not leave his face while he is
talking. He also STANDS PERFECTLY STRAIGHT at all times — he never leans,
slouches, sits or shifts his weight.

His six guards follow in matching uniform, a small gold V at cap and chest.

He walks past every guest without looking at any of them, and STOPS IN FRONT OF
THE ELDERLY GENTLEMAN IN THE WHITE SUIT — small, elderly, sharp-nosed, white
English tailoring, a black cane with a gold grip, a diamond ring, two bodyguards
behind him.

    "Sir. You should have told me you were coming."
    "I would have had the room ready for you."

The old man smiles — A FLASH OF TWO GOLD TEETH — and does not answer.

Valder sweeps one hand slowly across the whole room, WITHOUT TURNING TOWARD THE
FAR WALL.

    "Then let me show you what you are standing in."

DUPE is cleaning at the far edge of frame, back to the camera.

Camera: one slow lateral track following Valder in, settling as he stops. Level,
no handheld.

Colour grade — warm shadow, cold white: amber-orange highlights and mids, whites
pushed slightly cool, shadows never pure black but deep red-brown, saturation
high in flat planes but never touching skin, halation blooming around every lamp.
Photographed, not rendered: fine film grain, halation, faint gate weave, slight
colour fringing.

Valder walks the room, standing straight, smiling, introducing his collection.
The camera pushes in on each object as he names it, then returns to him.

THE MUSTARD SWIVEL ARMCHAIR on its blue patterned rug:

    "This chair was in the room where the armistice was signed. Not at the table.
    In the corner. Nobody sat in it."

THE LARGE WOVEN OVAL FORM hanging on wires:

    "A fish trap. It caught nothing for forty years. The man who made it never
    sold a single one."

THE UPRIGHT STONE RING on its low plinth:

    "A millstone. It ground grain for two hundred years. Then somebody stood it
    upright. And it stopped being a tool."

Guests follow him, listening. Nobody interrupts.

CRITICAL: PEOPLE AND A CHROMIUM COLUMN BLOCK THE FAR WALL THROUGHOUT. Valder
never sees the crack, never glances at it, and the camera never gives him a
clear line to it.

Camera: a push in on each object, then back to Valder. Level, unhurried.

SCENE 8c — THE SIXTH STORY · 25s · THE PIVOT OF THE FILM
===============================================================
References: loc_hall_big_d · char_valder · char_gentleman · char_woman ·
char_critic_b · char_oldman · char_cleaner_c

Continue exactly where 8b ended. Valder, hand still raised, facing the bare wall
with the crack and the brass plaque. The painting is gone.

HIS SMILE DOES NOT CHANGE. He does not flinch, does not blink hard, does not
look at anyone.

HE IS SILENT — two seconds, three. The hand stays up in the air. The whole room
waits. Nobody moves. This silence is the most important beat in the scene; hold
it.

Then he speaks on, IN EXACTLY THE SAME VOICE HE USED FOR THE OTHER FIVE — same
pace, same warmth, same certainty:

    "And this one."
    "I did not commission it. I did not buy it."
    "It happened to my wall while nobody was looking."
    "Every other thing in this room, someone decided to make."
    "That one decided by itself."

A pause.

    "I have been collecting for forty years to find something that did not need
    me."

THE RAISED HAND LOWERS, SLOWLY.

He is not lying and must never be played as lying. Every word is true; he has
only rearranged them. By the last line he believes it himself.

Camera: static on him, or the slowest possible push in. Nothing showy — the
performance carries it.

Same colour grade and film treatment as Scene 7.

25s · 720p · 16:9 · High · Sound On.

SCENE 9 — DUPE HEARS IT · 20s · HE SAYS NOTHING
===============================================================
References: loc_hall_big_d · char_cleaner_c · prop_cart

VALDER'S DIALOGUE FROM SCENE 8c RUNS CONTINUOUSLY OVER THIS ENTIRE SHOT, heard
from the next part of the hall — same words, same take, unbroken, slightly
distant and reverberant. Valder himself is never in frame.

DUPE is mopping at the edge of the room, his back against the shadow of a
chromium column, his cart beside him. THE REAL PAINTING IS LEANING AGAINST THE
FRONT OF HIS CART, facing outward.

    "…It happened to my wall while nobody was looking."

HIS HANDS STOP.

    "…Every other thing in this room, someone decided to make."

SWEAT RUNS DOWN HIS TEMPLE. He does not wipe it.

He looks at the painting on his cart. Then back toward the crowd.

    "…That one decided by itself."

HE OPENS HIS MOUTH.

One second.

HE CLOSES IT.

He looks down and goes back to mopping. Same rhythm, same speed as before.

    "…something that did not need me."

Camera: static, medium. No push, no handheld.

Same colour grade and film treatment as Scene 7.
