DIRECTION — THE CAMERA GRAMMAR (Wes Anderson pass, 2026-08-28)
Applies to every scene in this file. The dialogue and the actions
are LOCKED — this pass changes only how they are photographed.
================================================================
- SYMMETRY FIRST. Every composition is built around the vertical
  centreline of the frame. The hall's RED DOOR sits on that axis;
  when the door is visible it is dead centre at the vanishing point.
- PLANIMETRIC STAGING. The camera stands exactly perpendicular to
  the back wall, or exactly parallel to it. People are arranged
  facing the camera, or in exact profile — never three-quarter.
- THE CAMERA MOVES LIKE FURNITURE. Only these moves exist:
  a straight lateral dolly at constant speed, a slow push-in dead
  on the axis, a single fast 90-degree whip pan where marked, a
  single fast snap zoom where marked, and a locked-off tableau.
  Never handheld, never dutch, never drifting.
- ONE FIXED HEIGHT PER SHOT — waist height or eye height, chosen
  once, never changing inside the shot.
- DEADPAN TIMING. After anything happens, the frame HOLDS one full
  beat longer than is comfortable before the scene moves on.
- GROUP SHOTS ARE CLASS PHOTOS. People stand in neat rows facing
  camera, spaced evenly, alternating warm and cool costume colours.
- INSERTS ARE PORTRAITS OF OBJECTS: dead centre, frontal or exactly
  overhead (God's eye), lit flat, held still.

SEEDANCE 2.5 — 20 SECONDS — 720p — 16:9

THIS SCENE IS **ONE LONG TAKE, THEN ONE JUMP CUT, THEN A SECOND LONG TAKE.** That is all. There is exactly ONE cut in the entire twenty seconds. No other cuts, no dissolve, no fade, no wipe, no whip pan, no morph.

**DUPE SPEAKS. NOBODY ELSE DOES, AND NOBODY EVER ANSWERS HIM.**

He says short, polite, ordinary service phrases — because he needs to clean the spot where somebody is standing, or needs to get past them with the cart. **Every line is spoken quietly, warmly and politely, in English, and every single one goes unanswered.** Not one visitor replies, turns, nods, or moves. He is never annoyed by this and never repeats himself with any edge.

**These are his exact lines, in this order, and no others:**

1. **"Excuse me, sir."**
2. **"Sorry, madam."**
3. **"Excuse me. Just a moment."**
4. **"Sorry, sir."**
5. **"Thank you, sir."**

The last one is said to a man who has not moved and has not acknowledged him.

**NO OTHER DIALOGUE EXISTS IN THIS SCENE.** Nobody argues, nobody gathers, no visitor says anything at all. No subtitles, no captions, no voiceover.

**THE CAMERA IS ON DUPE THE WHOLE TIME.** He is the subject of every frame. Everyone else is scenery he moves through.

================================================================
WHAT THIS SCENE IS DOING
================================================================

**A man cleans a museum, and he is happy doing it.** That is the whole scene. There is no incident, no conversation, no drama.

Two things are being planted, both silently.

**ONE — WE MEET DUPE BEFORE ANYTHING HAPPENS TO HIM.** Twenty seconds of a man taking real pleasure in ordinary work. He dusts a frame and steps back to check it. He polishes a vitrine and catches his own reflection in it, satisfied. He is careful with things that are not his.

**TWO — HE SPEAKS TO PEOPLE AND NOBODY ANSWERS.** He is not silent and he is not sneaking around them. He says excuse me, he says sorry, he says thank you — five times, politely, to six people — and **not one reply comes back.** Not a nod, not a glance, not a step aside. This is never pointed out and he never reacts to it. It is simply how the room works. **He is more invisible for having spoken than he would have been in silence.**

**The film is called «Sorry, Sir» and this is where the title comes from.** It is not something anyone says to Valder. It is what Dupe says all day to people who do not hear him.

The scene is warm, and quietly funny, and it must be **completely sincere**. The camera does not wink. Nothing here is played as a joke.

================================================================
STYLE
================================================================

ERA: a retrofuturist world — the future as people in the past imagined it. Swept aerodynamic curves, moulded plastic, chromium, terrazzo, tapered splayed legs, starburst and orbital forms. **NOTHING DIGITAL ANYWHERE** — no screens, no LEDs, no digital numerals, no cables.

COLOUR: wealth is DEEP SATURATED COLOUR IN LARGE FLAT PLANES. Every visitor wears ONE saturated colour and no two wear the same one. **NOBODY WEARS BLACK.**

COLOUR GRADE — **warm shadow, cold white**: amber-orange highlights and mids, whites pushed slightly cool, **shadows never black but deep red-brown**, saturation high in the flat planes but **never touching skin**, halation blooming around every lamp.

THE LIGHT IS WARM AND BRIGHT, never moody, never dim.

THE CAMERA IS ALWAYS LEVEL. No dutch angles, no tilting.

PHOTOGRAPHED, NOT RENDERED: fine film grain, halation around bright highlights, faint gate weave, slight softness and colour fringing at the edges of the lens. The grain must NOT desaturate the colour. NO HDR, NO digital glossiness, NO CGI sheen.

**EVERY CHARACTER APPEARS EXACTLY ONCE. No duplicates, no twins, no repeated faces.**
