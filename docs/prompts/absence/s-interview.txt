THE CLOSING INTERVIEW · five clips · CEO 2026-09-03

Dupe, rich, telling the story himself. We never hear the interviewer and never
see them. Every answer implies its question.

The engine of the sequence, and of the film: he is telling the exact truth in
plain words, and everyone hearing it mistakes it for profundity. He is not being
modest and he is not being sly. He is being accurate, and nobody in the room can
hear it. Play every line flat.

Plate: @project_absence_char_dupe_interview_house (created 2026-09-03, $0.06,
derived from @project_absence_char_cleaner_rich for face continuity). One frame
carries all five clips.

Word budget: our measured ceiling is 45-50 spoken words per 20s clip. Counts per
clip below are 38 / 30 / 35 / 38 / 50.

CTO edit to the CEO's text, flagged rather than made silently: four filler words
removed from clip 5 to bring it from 54 to 50 — "anywhere from" and "that day".
Meaning unchanged. Everything else is the CEO's wording verbatim.

Clip 2 is the only clip whose camera moves. Placement was chosen so the gag
lands on character rather than slapstick: the line before it is about dropping
something by accident, the line after it is about being afraid of the
consequences, and the crash makes the second line read in two tenses at once —
he is describing the past and flinching in the present. He is rich and he still
jumps when he hears something break.
