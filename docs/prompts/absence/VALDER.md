# VALDER — the man, his five pieces, and the improvisation

CEO brief, 2026-08-28. Dialogue written by the CTO per the standing rule that
no model invents a line.

---

## THE MAN

Look is **locked** — reuse the existing plate, asset
`6edafc56-5dff-4c52-844e-73b125e047f0`, filed as `project_absence_char_valder`.
Tall, very thin, elderly. Silver hair swept straight back. Small round tinted
sunglasses. A suit jacket panelled in magenta, yellow, green, blue and red over
a scarlet waistcoat. Purple trousers. A long mustard scarf. **Deliberately
mismatched shoes — one green, one oxblood.**

**Why this look is right, and it must never be explained on screen:** the rule
of this world is that wealth is one saturated colour per person. Valder wears
*every* colour at once, and two different shoes. He is not a rank inside the
colour system. He is the man who set it.

### How he behaves — CEO, verbatim

- **He smiles the entire time he speaks.** Not playful, not warm. **A still,
  deadpan smile** that never changes and never reaches the eyes. It is simply
  the shape his face makes.
- **He stands perfectly straight at all times.** He does not lean, slouch, sit
  or shift his weight. Ever.
- He has **a fair amount of dialogue** in Phase 2, when he arrives.
- He is **not yet very famous** — but famous enough that a museum exists showing
  nothing but his own collection. The audience learns in this scene that **he
  owns the place.**

## PHASE 2 — THE SCENE

Valder walks in and takes over the room. He explains his pieces one at a time,
five of them, and the camera pushes in on each as he does.

**He does not know what has happened.** He believes the crowd at the far wall is
looking at a painting — because it *should* be a painting; that is what hangs
there. **From where he stands the crack is hidden from him** — by the people in
front of it, by one of the chromium columns, by the angle. He never sees it.

He finishes the five. He turns to the sixth and last piece, the one just bid up
to **$20,000,000** — and finds **the painting gone, and every person in the room
staring at a crack in the plaster.**

He does not miss a beat. **He improvises.** Standing straight, smiling, he
invents the story of how the crack came to be and why it is worth what it is
worth. It is the sixth story of the scene and it sounds exactly like the other
five, because he has told five already and the audience has learned the grammar.

**That is the trap the film is built on:** by the time he lies, we have been
trained to accept the shape of the lie.

## THE FIVE PIECES

Chosen from objects **already present in the hall plate** (`loc_hall_big_d`) so
the room stays continuous. Mixed kinds as instructed, no more than two of any
one kind: **one painting · one chair · two sculptures · one small artefact.**

Each gets its own plate so the camera can push in and the model can render real
detail.

Every acquisition story runs on the same hidden rule: **the value never comes
from the object.** It comes from a story, an accident, an absence, or a
rotation. Five variations of that — and then Valder does it a sixth time, to a
crack in a wall.

---

### 1 · THE CHAIR — `project_absence_prop_valder_chair`

**In the room:** the mustard-yellow swivel armchair on its blue and grey
patterned rug, the one with a clear sightline to the hero wall.

**Plate:** the chair alone, three-quarter front, on the blue rug. Retrofuturist
moulded shell on a four-star chrome swivel base, buttoned mustard upholstery,
worn very slightly on the left arm where a hand has rested for years. Warm
gallery light, terracotta terrazzo underfoot, plain beyond. No people.

**Valder:** *"This chair was in the room where the armistice was signed."*
*"Not at the table. In the corner."*
*"Nobody sat in it."*

**Why it earns its place:** value by proximity to an event, with no
participation in it. That is precisely what will happen to the crack — a plaque
sits near it, so it becomes art. He states the film's thesis in the first thirty
seconds and nobody notices, including him.

---

### 2 · THE HANGING WOVEN FORM — `project_absence_prop_valder_trap`

**In the room:** the large oval lattice basket suspended from the ceiling on
thin wires.

**Plate:** the woven form alone, hung on its wires against the pale ceiling, lit
from below by the orange coves so the weave throws a shadow. Split cane and
willow, hand-lashed, dry with age, one rib repaired in darker wood. Big — the
size of a man. No people.

**Valder:** *"A fish trap."*
*"It caught nothing for forty years."*
*"The man who made it never sold a single one."*

**Why:** value in total failure. The object is defined by what it did not do —
exactly what "The Absence of Meaning" will be.

---

### 3 · THE STONE RING — `project_absence_prop_valder_millstone`

**In the room:** the large stone torus standing upright on a low stone plinth,
its surface built of small stacked blocks.

**Plate:** the ring alone on its plinth, lit from one side so the mosaic of
blocks reads as texture. Grey-brown stone, worn smooth on the inner face,
chipped at one edge. Human height. Terracotta floor. No people.

**Valder:** *"A millstone. It ground grain for two hundred years."*
*"Then somebody stood it upright."*
*"And it stopped being a tool."*

**Why:** value created by rotation. Nothing about the object changed — only its
orientation. The cheapest possible transformation, sold as transfiguration.

---

### 4 · THE LIDDED VESSEL — `project_absence_prop_valder_vessel`

**In the room:** the dark bronze-brown lidded urn with a spire finial, on its
stepped plinth in the foreground of the wide hall.

**Plate:** the vessel alone on its plinth. Dark glazed ceramic with a metallic
sheen, heavy ribbed body, domed lid rising to a sharp finial, standing on a
darker stepped base. Warm light raking across so the glaze breaks into
highlights. No people.

**Valder:** *"Seventeen of these were made."*
*"He destroyed sixteen."*
*"He kept the one he liked least."*

**Why:** rarity manufactured by destruction, and the survivor is the worst one.
The audience laughs — and then the same logic is used on them.

---

### 5 · THE PAINTING — `project_absence_prop_valder_study`

**In the room:** one of the richly textured framed works on the white wall.

**Plate:** the painting alone on a white wall, thin dark frame, heavy impasto in
ochre, rust, sage and cream, thickly worked, visible brush drag and one long
scrape where a blade has pulled through the paint. Completely invented — no
recognisable style, no figures, nothing anyone could attribute. Warm gallery
light, halation at the edge. No people.

**Valder:** *"He painted this in one afternoon, to test a colour."*
*"The finished work is in a vault in Geneva."*
*"Nobody has ever asked to see it."*

**Why:** the study outranks the work. The last and most direct rehearsal for
what he is about to do — a thing never meant to be the art becomes the art,
because he says so.

---

## THE SIXTH — improvised, no plate

He turns to the wall expecting the painting, and finds the crack.

He does not hesitate and his smile does not change. He tells a sixth story in
the same grammar as the five: an accident, an absence, a rotation, a refusal.

**The line is not written yet — it is the pivot of the film and belongs to the
CEO.** What it has to do: sound exactly like the other five, be obviously
invented to us and obviously true to them, and never once acknowledge that he
is looking at damage.
