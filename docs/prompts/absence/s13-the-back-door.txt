---------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS IS THE BEST SCENE VALDER GETS
---------------------------------------------------------------
All film he performs that warm ingratiating charm **in front of an audience**.
This is the one time he uses it with nobody watching — and it works exactly the
same. That is what tells us the charm was never a performance. It is simply what
he is.

He is not covering up a crime. **He believes it.** By now he genuinely thinks the
crack is the finest thing he owns, so to him this is not a bribe, it is a rescue.
He thinks he is being kind. That is what makes it funny, and what keeps him from
becoming a villain.

**The workman is the only person in the film who cannot be bought.** Dupe is the
only one who tells the truth; the workman is the only one who refuses money. They
are the two poorest people on screen — and nobody ever says so out loud.

---------------------------------------------------------------
THE PLACE
---------------------------------------------------------------
**EXTERIOR. THE BACK OF THE MUSEUM.** Not the grand front — the service side:
plain concrete, exposed pipework, a steel service door, a delivery bay, two bins.
The beautiful building seen from behind, where nobody photographs it.

Warm afternoon light, long shadows. Traffic somewhere far off.

**THEY ARE THE ONLY TWO PEOPLE IN FRAME.** Nobody passes. Nobody watches.

VALDER stands perfectly straight in the panelled jacket, absurdly overdressed
against the concrete, **smiling that warm ingratiating smile the entire time.**

THE WORKMAN is in his slate-blue overalls, still holding his bucket and float. He
has been walked out here and he does not know why.

---------------------------------------------------------------
THE SCENE
---------------------------------------------------------------
    VALDER: "Do you know what you were about to do?"

    WORKMAN: "…Fill a crack. Sir."

    VALDER: "Yes."

A beat. Valder's smile does not move.

    VALDER: "How much were you offered?"

    WORKMAN: "Eight hundred."

    VALDER: "I will give you eight thousand."

The workman looks down at his bucket. Then back up.

    WORKMAN: "To fix it?"

    VALDER: "To have never come."

**HOLD ON THE WORKMAN.** He thinks about it — genuinely, for a second, because
eight thousand is real money to him.

Then he shakes his head. Once. Not angry. Almost apologetic.

    WORKMAN: "Someone asked me to fix it."

He picks up his bucket and **walks back in through the service door**, past
Valder, who does not stop him.

**END ON VALDER, alone against the concrete, still smiling.** The smile has not
changed once in the whole scene. Hold two seconds. Cut.

HOW IT HANDS OFF
---------------------------------------------------------------
Because he refuses, the workman **still walks to the wall in S14** with his
bucket and float, which is what brings Dupe running in S15. **The refusal is the
thing that keeps the story moving** — if he took the money, the film stops here.
