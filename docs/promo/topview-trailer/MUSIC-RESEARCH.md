# Film-score research: Doctor Sleep (2019) & The Shining (1980)

Compiled 2026-09-26 for CTO music-generation prompt writing. Read-only research,
no generation, no spend. Every claim below has a source URL; anything backed by
only one non-official source is flagged "(single source)".

---

## 1. The Shining (1980) — Wendy Carlos & Rachel Elkind + licensed avant-garde pieces

**The Dies Irae main title**
- Carlos and Elkind based the Main Title on the medieval "Dies Irae" chant
  (13th-century, traditionally used in funeral liturgy — "day of wrath"),
  synthesized in low-register brass tone, set in B♭ / Dorian mode, at a very
  slow tempo of roughly **56 BPM**, with the chant itself in steady, plodding
  quarter notes.
  Source: https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/horror-music-from-the-shining-main-title-explained/
- Between phrases of the chant, Carlos/Elkind built "ambient collages" of
  highly processed vocals and exotic percussion layered over a **sustained
  synthesized low drone** (a B♭ pedal); these passages are rhythmically free,
  with no steady pulse, using chromaticism/microtonal pitch-bending outside
  equal temperament to sound "creepy and strange."
  Source: https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/horror-music-from-the-shining-main-title-explained/

**The three synth cues actually used in the film**
- Only three of Carlos/Elkind's cues made the final cut: (1) the Dies Irae
  Main Title; (2) "Rocky Mountains," an atmospheric transition cue for the
  Torrances' drive to the Overlook; (3) "Shining/Heartbeat," described as
  functioning "more in the realm of sound than music," recurring three times
  in a twenty-minute span to represent the psychic "shine" link between Danny
  and Dick Halloran.
  Source: https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2020/the-shining-at-40/reappraising-the-score-of-the-shining/
- Carlos built/commissioned custom synth capability for the film (described
  as a "singing" synth) and had pushed Robert Moog for innovations like
  filter banks and pitch-sliding controls used across her scores.
  Source: https://www.abc.net.au/listen/classic/read-and-watch/music-reads/tron-the-shining-what-makes-wendy-carlos-music-so-great/102061374

**Licensed 20th-century orchestral pieces (chosen/edited in by Kubrick and music editor Gordon Stainforth)**
- Krzysztof Penderecki: several pieces including "De Natura Sonoris" No. 1 and
  No. 2, "Polymorphia," "Kanon," "Utrenja" I & II, "The Dream/Awakening of
  Jacob" — heaviest in the film's final third.
  Source: https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2020/the-shining-at-40/reappraising-the-score-of-the-shining/
- De Natura Sonoris' defining techniques: **quarter-tone tone clusters**
  (dense adjacent-note groupings), **glissandi** (slides), whistling string
  harmonics, and unusual extended percussion (an iron bar struck with a
  hammer/saw in No. 2).
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Natura_Sonoris
- György Ligeti's "Lontano" (dense micropolyphonic string/orchestral
  clusters) and Béla Bartók's "Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta"
  round out the modernist palette.
  Source: https://www.resistormag.com/music/soundtrack-the-shining-stanley-kubrick/
- None of the licensed pieces were used in their original unedited form —
  Stainforth cut and layered fragments (sometimes multiple pieces stacked)
  to fit specific sequences, so the film's perceived music is a distortion of
  each original composition.
  Source: https://www.resistormag.com/music/soundtrack-the-shining-stanley-kubrick/
- Sonic architecture of the film operates on three levels: pre-existent
  avant-garde concert music (horror/tension score), 1930s big-band songs
  played diegetically in the Gold Room (melodic contrast), and Carlos's
  synth cues bridging the two — the synth's "uncanny" timbre widens the gap
  between "sound" and "music" and heightens unease.
  Source: https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2020/the-shining-at-40/reappraising-the-score-of-the-shining/

## 2. Doctor Sleep (2019) — The Newton Brothers

**Referencing Carlos's score directly**
- The Doctor Sleep score explicitly quotes/reworks the 1980 Main Title: the
  soundtrack track "Dies Irae / Violet" incorporates "The Shining" main title
  theme, and "The Overlook" is described as an epic reprisal of Carlos's
  theme.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Sleep_(soundtrack)
- Director Mike Flanagan asked for the same "impenetrable sense of dread"
  Kubrick created via score/sound design, but wanted it "bigger, with
  interesting sounds," woven into its own musical landscape rather than
  simply repeated.
  Source: https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
- Critic Clarisse Loughrey noted Carlos/Elkind's original score — "those
  electronic notes that sound like foghorns" — only appears once in Doctor
  Sleep, i.e. it's a deliberate single, precise quotation rather than a
  constant callback. (single source)
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Sleep_(soundtrack)

**Instruments and recording methods**
- A **90-foot Aeolian wind harp** was recorded outdoors in San Francisco at
  varying wind speeds to capture low howling tones and high "weird" whistling
  overtones.
  Source: https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
- A **20-foot "Hurdy Gurdy Grandé"** (giant hurdy-gurdy) was built/recorded
  specifically for Rose the Hat and the True Knot cult, its drone mixed
  against an "off-kilter" heartbeat pulse to create a driving, compulsive
  feeling.
  Source: https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/newton-brothers-doctor-sleep-difficulty-190718976.html
- Ancient/ethnic percussion, bass flutes (pitched down in processing),
  chimes, tubular bells and bowls were each assigned to specific characters —
  e.g. wind-harp textures track Danny's uncontrollable torment, wind chimes
  track Abra's controlled command of her abilities — so instrumentation does
  character-specific tone-painting rather than a single running theme.
  Source: https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
- Taylor Stewart (of the Newton Brothers) used **modular synthesizer gear**
  extensively to process vocal recordings into "weird sounds throughout the
  film" — some organic recordings were left raw for contrast.
  Source: https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/

**Structure and harmony**
- The score deliberately avoids traditional hummable melody, using a
  **12-tone-derived, dissonant structure** to stay "tonally driven" toward
  dread rather than melody-driven.
  Source: https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
- The composers studied Kubrick's original cue sheet and noted how multiple
  classical-era pieces were layered chaotically on top of each other in the
  1980 film — an approach they carried into their own layering choices.
  Source: https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
- Orchestration was spatialized: the violin section was split into three
  groups so melodic lines trade mid-phrase between them, keeping "nothing...
  stagnant," with sound placed "all over the room."
  Source: https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
- General Newton Brothers method (confirmed for their other Flanagan scores,
  same duo/vocabulary): minimal/restrained instrumentation, cues recorded
  rubato per-scene rather than to a click track for human-feel timing, heavy
  use of dissonance with deliberate silence between sparse notes.
  Source: https://www.popdisciple.com/interviews/the-newton-brothers

**The heartbeat as the score's signature device**
- The interview framing itself — "the Difficulty of Scoring to a Heartbeat" —
  and reviewer Angelica Jade Bastién's line "A score by the Newton Brothers
  thumps like an errant heartbeat" both identify the heartbeat pulse as the
  score's most recognisable device, not a one-off cue.
  Source: https://consequence.net/2019/11/episode-142-doctor-sleep-movie/ and
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Sleep_(soundtrack)

## Signature techniques to imitate (the 6–10 that make it sound like Doctor Sleep/The Shining)

1. **A slow, physical sub-bass heartbeat pulse** used as a driving/dread
   device, sometimes deliberately "off-kilter" rather than metronomic.
   https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/newton-brothers-doctor-sleep-difficulty-190718976.html
2. **A Dies Irae–style four-note-ish descending chant figure**, stated
   plainly in low synthesized brass/drone register at a very slow tempo
   (~56 BPM), steady quarter notes, no ornamentation.
   https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/horror-music-from-the-shining-main-title-explained/
3. **A sustained low analog synth drone** (a single held pedal tone) under
   everything else, the harmonic anchor for both scores.
   https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/horror-music-from-the-shining-main-title-explained/
4. **Processed breath/vocal textures** — heavily treated human vocal or
   breathing sounds folded into the drone rather than sung melody.
   https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/horror-music-from-the-shining-main-title-explained/
5. **Dense dissonant string clusters and glissandi** (quarter-tone clusters,
   slides, whistling harmonics) borrowed from the avant-garde orchestral
   vocabulary, used for tension rather than tune.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Natura_Sonoris
6. **Rhythmically free, near-pulseless "sound" passages** that alternate with
   the steady-pulse chant/heartbeat sections — dread comes from the contrast.
   https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/horror-music-from-the-shining-main-title-explained/
7. **Non-melodic, 12-tone-derived dissonant harmony** — deliberately avoiding
   a hummable theme in favor of tone color and dread.
   https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
8. **Unconventional physical/acoustic sound sources treated electronically**
   (giant wind harp, giant hurdy-gurdy, bowed metal bowls, pitched-down bass
   flute) rather than a clean synth-only or orchestra-only palette.
   https://www.flickeringmyth.com/exclusive-interview-doctor-sleep-composers-the-newton-brothers-talk-mike-flanagan-and-the-shining/
9. **Terraced/stepped dynamics** — sudden loud swells dropping to near
   silence, rather than a smooth continuous crescendo — used both in the
   Penderecki-derived material and the new score.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Natura_Sonoris (technique) /
   https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2020/the-shining-at-40/reappraising-the-score-of-the-shining/ (usage in-film)
10. **A single, precise quotation of the earlier score's theme** rather than
    constant callback — reworking/reprising it once at a structurally
    important moment (e.g. "The Overlook").
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Sleep_(soundtrack)
