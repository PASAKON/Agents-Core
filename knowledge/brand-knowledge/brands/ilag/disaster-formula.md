# ILAG disaster short: our formula (FilmFormula M2, 29 Oct 2026)

For ILAG YouTube, Western audience. CEO picks: disaster + true-disaster series (Q-20261029-0719-c40b);
main models Sydney Mega Tsunami and the 1761 shipwreck film (Q-20261029-0725-ab36).
Learned from structure only. Other people's films are ideas, never our base: no dialogue, scene order,
characters, places or titles of theirs go into ours. Evidence: docs/reports/filmformula/M2-report.md.

## Two formats
| | F1 Disaster drama (fiction) | F2 True disaster (narrated) |
|---|---|---|
| Model | A: tsunami, 11.8 min, 605k views | B: shipwreck, 21.6 min, 2.9M views |
| Length | 10-13 min | 18-22 min |
| Voice | short shouted lines, ~31 words/min | one narrator, ~143 words/min, never stops |
| Picture | fast spectacle, many locations | slow, mostly still wides and close details |
| Ending | cliffhanger "Part 2" | rescue + what happened after, "the place today" |
| AI risk | lip-sync and acting in many short lines | almost none: no lip-sync needed |

F2 is the cheaper and safer first pilot: narration hides AI's weak acting, and the model film had ~5x the views (2.9M vs 605k).
Assumed: one film per format, so this is a hint, not proof.

## F1 beat sheet (percent of runtime)
| % | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Hook | a strange sign of the disaster, no dialogue, in the first 5 s |
| 2-14 | Cause | experts or instruments explain it in 2-3 lines; the order to run |
| ~12 | Want | one family line that sets the hero's flaw or promise |
| 14-28 | Inciting | someone the hero loves is in the danger zone |
| ~28 | Title card | after the cold open, not before |
| 28-42 | Set piece 1 | the wave/fire hits landmarks; spectacle, little talk |
| 44-58 | Set piece 2 = emotional peak | the hero saves the loved one, hand-to-hand rescue |
| ~59-70 | Turn | a human villain makes it worse (greed, rank, power) |
| ~76 | Peak threat | a bigger wave/fire is announced; scale is spoken as a number |
| 77-96 | Escalation | 3+ hazards in a row, short shots |
| 97-100 | Cliffhanger | "end of part one", series hook |

## F2 beat sheet (chapters, percent of runtime)
| % | Chapter | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0-4 | Cold open | the place today + the promise ("nobody remembers them") |
| ~5 | Date card | a real date and place on screen |
| 4-14 | The disaster | how it happened, 2 min |
| 14-29 | Betrayal / abandonment | the first turn: help leaves |
| 29-40 | Survival basics | water, fire, food, shelter: one problem per scene |
| 40-55 | Midpoint build | a setback (storm) then they build something lasting |
| 55-73 | Failed hope | escape attempts that fail; losses |
| 73-88 | Dark years | time jumps, fewer people |
| ~86 | Arrival | the rescuer, named |
| 88-100 | Rescue + aftermath | who lived, what came after |
8 chapters, each 2-4 min, each titled in the YouTube chapters.

## Pace numbers
- Shot length (from CC-BY Tears of Steel, our live-action baseline): average 4.7 s, median 3.0 s.
  F1 action scenes: 2-3 s; F2: 4-8 s holds with slow camera moves.
- Speech: F1 ~31 words/min with long silent spectacle; F2 ~143 words/min narration.
- Scenes: F1 ~1.1 beats/min across ~10 locations; F2 1 chapter per 2.7 min.

## What AI did well / badly in these films (by eye, storyboard frames)
- Well: wide disaster shots (waves over landmarks, a ship on a reef), weather (hail, lightning, aurora),
  still survival details (fire, stone walls, water jars).
- Badly: faces change between shots in F1 crowd and rescue scenes; text on screens; many black or soft
  frames between clips. F2 avoids this by staying wide and narrated.

## Our rules for ILAG
1. Our own event, place, people and title. A real disaster (F2) is told from public history; say what is
   fiction (F1 must say the event is fictional if it uses a real city).
2. Hook in 5 s with picture only. Title after the cold open.
3. One loved person in danger, one human villain (F1); one group and one betrayal (F2).
4. Write for Flow: every shot a self-contained prompt; keep faces few and wide in F1.
5. F2 real events: tick YouTube's "altered or synthetic content" disclosure on every upload (a realistic AI
   re-enactment of a real event needs it), and put "AI re-enactment" in the description and on the date card (CMO 2026-10-29).
6. Real victims: no gore, no mocking, no invented names for real people who died. Use real names only from public
   history and cite the source in the description (CMO 2026-10-29).
